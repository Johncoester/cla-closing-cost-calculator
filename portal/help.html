1Welcome

This guide shows you how to use CLA's closing cost calculator and your saved quotes page.

PartWhat it is
Closing cost calculatorWorks out an estimate of closing costs for a purchase, a cash purchase or a refinance, and what a seller would walk away with. Anyone can use it, signed in or not.
Your quotes pageWhen you sign in, the quotes you run are saved to your account. This page lists them so you can open, print or delete them. It also shows your profile and your CLA rep.
Every number here is an estimate. It is not a Loan Estimate, a Closing Disclosure or a promise to insure. For a binding quote, ask your CLA rep.

Use your browser's Find (Ctrl+F, or Cmd+F on a Mac) to search this page. The pictures use made-up sample people and addresses.

2Signing in

There is no public sign-up. CLA sends you an email invitation, and you sign in with that same email address.

Your first time: the invitation email

  1. Open the email from CLA and click the linkThe link works for 24 hours. If it has lapsed, see "Link expired or forgot your password" below.
  2. Choose a passwordAt least 12 characters, with upper and lower case letters and a number. Type it twice.
  3. Accept the privacy notice and termsTick the box, then choose Create my account.
  4. Finish your profileAdd your name and company. A photo is optional. See "Your profile and photo".

Signing in after that

  1. Open the sign-in pageGo to quotes.clatitle.com/portal/login.html, or choose Log in at the top of the calculator.
  2. Enter your email and passwordUse the email address CLA invited. Choose Sign in.
  3. You land on your quotes pageFrom there choose New quote, or Calculator in the top bar, to run another.

Link expired or forgot your password

  1. Open the sign-in page and choose Forgot password?
  2. Enter your email and choose Send reset linkYou always see the same message, whether or not the email is on file: "If that email has a CLA portal account, a reset link is on its way." You can ask for another link after about a minute.
  3. Open the newest email and choose a new passwordReset links also last 24 hours. A used or expired link says "This link has expired or was already used. Request a new one from the sign-in page." Choose Forgot password? again for a fresh one.

To sign out, choose Sign out in the top bar.

3Your quotes page

Choose Dashboard in the top bar any time to come back here.

  • My profile: your name, company and contact details, with a link to edit them and your photo.
  • My CLA rep: your rep's name, photo and phone numbers (see "Your CLA rep").
  • Saved quotes: each quote with its type (Buyer quote or Seller net), price, the date saved, and a note of how long it has left.

Open, print or delete a quote

  1. Choose View / print on the quoteThe quote opens with your photo and your rep's photo at the top, the total, every section, and any warnings.
  2. Choose Print / Save PDF to keep a copyYour browser's print window opens. Pick Save as PDF to keep a file.
  3. To remove a quote, choose DeleteYou are asked to confirm. A deleted quote cannot be brought back.

Quotes delete themselves 30 days after they were created, and editing a quote does not restart that clock. Print or copy anything you want to keep longer. If you open a quote that is gone, you see "That quote was not found."

4Using the calculator

Follow the steps below. The estimate on the right updates with every change.

Step by step: a buyer quote

  1. Pick Buyer QuoteSeller Net Sheet is explained further down.
  2. Pick the type of transactionPurchase is a financed sale. Cash has no loan. Refinance replaces the sale price with the payoff of the old loan and the amount of the new one.
  3. Enter the ZIP codeThe state and county fill in by themselves, because county taxes depend on them. The address and city are optional and show on your quote.
  4. Check the county or cityYou can change it in the County / city list.
  5. Enter the price and the loan amountOn a refinance, enter the payoff and the new loan amount.
  6. Choose the policy optionsOwner's policy, rate, who pays, and how fees are shown.
  7. Tick endorsements and check the documentsBoth are under Fees & options.
  8. Read the estimate on the rightThen print it, copy it, or let it save.

The inputs in plain words

InputWhat it does
Purchase / Cash / RefinancePurchase: a financed sale. Cash: no loan, so no lender's policy and no lender-only fees. Refinance: a payoff and a new loan, with no owner's policy and no seller column.
Property address, CityOptional. They print on your quote and appear on the saved quote.
ZIPFive digits. It fills in the state and county. If a ZIP covers more than one county, the page says so. If a ZIP is not on file, you pick the state and county yourself (CLA covers MD, VA, DC and FL).
State, County / cityMD, DC, VA or FL. The county or city decides county recording fees and transfer taxes. Until you choose one, the page says "Enter a ZIP or choose a county to include county taxes."
UnderwriterThe title insurance company whose rates are used.
Sale price, Payoff, Loan amountThe price is not used on a refinance. Payoff and loan show on a refinance. Loan is hidden on a cash purchase.
Owner's policyEnhanced / Homeowner's or Standard coverage.
RateBasic, or Reissue (a policy on the property already exists, which can lower the premium).
Owner's policy paid byLocal custom (what is usual in that county), Buyer or Seller.
Fee presentationLoan Estimate / CD (TRID) shows premiums the way the lender's forms do. Actual premium split shows what each person really pays.
First-time homebuyerApplies a first-time buyer rate or exemption where the county has one. Not used on a refinance.
Owner-occupied principal residenceOn by default. Some exemptions depend on it. In Maryland, a long deed (10 or more pages) that is not a principal residence records for $115 instead of $60, and a note says so.
Buyer pays seller's transfer / deed taxA contract term, for example Florida deed stamps. It moves the seller's tax to the buyer column.
Documents to recordHow many Deeds, Deeds of Trust, Releases and Powers of Attorney, and how many pages each. The starting numbers match a typical CLA quote.
EndorsementsOptional extra coverage. A few are ticked to begin with. The choices depend on the state, the underwriter and the type of transaction.
CLA feesLocked. They come straight from CLA's current fee list and are tagged Buyer or Seller.

What each line in your estimate means

Lines are grouped under letters: B Services you cannot shop for, C Services you can shop for, E Taxes and other government fees, H Other. Each group has a buyer column (the borrower, on a refinance), a seller column and a total.

LineWhat it is
Title - Lender's Title PolicyInsurance that protects the lender, based on the loan amount. Only when there is a loan.
Title - Owner's Title Policy (optional)Insurance that protects the buyer's ownership, based on the price. Not on a refinance. Marked "simultaneous-issue" when it is bought together with a loan policy.
Title - Endorsement ...Extra coverage added to a policy. A flat amount or a percentage of the premium.
Title - Settlement Fee to CLA Title & EscrowCLA's fee for handling the closing. It can differ for the buyer, the seller and a refinance.
Other CLA feesDocument preparation, notary, secure document storage, title review and data collection, title search and abstract, closing protection letter (lender only), and electronic recording.
Recording fees - Deed, Deed of Trust, Release, Power of AttorneyWhat the county charges to record each document. Shown as "x2" and so on when there is more than one.
Transfer and recordation taxesState, county or city taxes on the sale or the loan. Who pays follows local custom and your contract; some are split.
Warnings boxA yellow box that appears when something could not be priced, for example no recording fee found for a county. Treat the total as incomplete until you understand the warning, and ask your CLA rep.

Reading the results

The big number is the buyer total (the borrower total on a refinance), with the seller total and the all-parties total underneath. Each section lists its lines, and an Estimate total closes the page. The line under the total names the transaction, county, state and underwriter. If it says BUILT-IN SNAPSHOT, the page could not reach CLA's latest fee list: choose Reload team sheet to try again.

Seller Net Sheet

Choose Seller Net Sheet to see what a seller keeps. Address, ZIP, state, county and price carry across when you switch.

  • Property and sale: address, ZIP, state, county, selling price and closing date.
  • Commissions: the buyer's broker and the seller's broker, each as a percent or a dollar amount.
  • Payoffs and property taxes: first mortgage payoff, second mortgage or HELOC, yearly property taxes and the date they are paid through. Taxes are shared by days: the seller owns every day up to the day before closing, using a 365-day year.
  • Credits and other costs: seller concession (dollars or percent), HOA dues owed, HOA transfer fee, HOA special assessment, home warranty, other fees, and up to five lines of your own, such as repairs.
  • Title and settlement: who pays the owner's policy, whether the buyer is a first-time homebuyer, whether the buyer pays the seller's transfer tax, and the CLA seller fees.
  • Result: net proceeds are the selling price minus the seller's total costs minus the mortgage payoffs.

Saving, copying and printing

  • While you are signed in, your quote saves by itself about 2.5 seconds after you change something. It keeps updating the same quote. Save as new quote makes a second copy. The Buyer and Seller tabs save as separate quotes.
  • The note under the buttons says "Saved to your account", shows the date the quote will delete itself (30 days after it was created), and links to View with photos and My quotes.
  • If you are not signed in, the button says Log in to save. After you sign in, a confirmation page saves the quote you were working on.
  • Copy quote puts a plain-text version on your clipboard to paste into an email. Print / Save PDF opens your browser's print window.

Your photo and your rep's photo on a quote

When you are signed in, a bar under the Buyer Quote / Seller Net Sheet switch shows your photo, name and company on one side, and your CLA rep's photo, name and phone number on the other, with "Prepared with C.L.A. Title & Escrow". It also prints. Until someone adds a photo, a plain placeholder shows.

A special rate

Sometimes CLA has arranged a special rate for you. When that is the case, the CLA fees list shows a lower settlement fee with a short note beside it, such as "(special rate, standard $1,075.00)". It is already counted in your total and you do not need to do anything. Printed, copied and saved quotes show the amount without the note. If you have a question about your rate, ask your CLA rep.

5Your profile and photo

Open your name in the top bar, or choose Edit profile and photo on your quotes page.

  • First and last name are required. Add your company so quotes carry your details.
  • Phone, company phone, company address, website and license / NMLS number are optional and print on your quotes.
  • Your cell phone is optional. It must be a US number, for example (240) 555-0142, and your CLA rep can see it.
  • To change the email you sign in with, contact CLA.

A photo is optional. Use a JPEG or PNG up to 2 MB. The portal crops it to a centered square and shrinks it to 512 pixels. iPhone HEIC photos are not accepted yet: take a screenshot of the photo or save it as a JPEG first. Choose Choose photo, then Upload this photo. Choose Remove photo to go back to the placeholder.

6Your CLA rep

The rep CLA has matched you with is your first call for any question about a quote.

  • Your quotes page has a My CLA rep box with your rep's name, photo, cell phone (tap it on a phone to call), office phone and email address (click it to write an email).
  • Your rep's name and number also print on your quotes.
  • If the box says you have not been matched to a CLA rep yet, your quotes still save, and CLA will connect you by email.

9Glossary

Plain-English meanings for the terms you will see.

TermMeaning
Owner's policyTitle insurance that protects the buyer's ownership.
Lender's policyTitle insurance that protects the lender's loan.
Simultaneous issueBuying both policies at once, which usually lowers the lender's premium.
EndorsementAn add-on that extends a policy's coverage.
Reissue rateA lower premium when a policy on the property already exists.
Settlement feeCLA's fee for handling the closing.
Recording feeWhat the county charges to record a document in the land records.
Transfer tax, recordation taxTaxes paid to the state or county when ownership or a loan is recorded.
Deed, Deed of Trust, ReleaseThe documents recorded for a sale, for a loan, and for paying off a loan.
Power of attorneyA document that lets someone sign for another person.
TRID, Loan Estimate, Closing DisclosureThe federal rules and lender forms that show closing costs.
CPLClosing protection letter, a lender-required assurance about the closing agent.
UnderwriterThe insurance company that issues the title policy.
Promulgated ratesRates set by the state (Florida) rather than by the company.
ProrationSplitting a recurring cost, such as property tax, by days between seller and buyer.
PayoffThe amount needed to close out an existing loan.
HELOCHome equity line of credit.
ConcessionA credit the seller gives the buyer.
Net sheetAn estimate of what the seller walks away with.
Principal residenceThe home the owner lives in as a main home.
Title search, abstractResearch into the property's ownership and any claims against it.
E-recordingRecording documents electronically.
